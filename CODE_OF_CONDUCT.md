# Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

We are committed to providing a welcoming, safe, and respectful environment for
everyone who participates in the Scouting OpenID Connect project, regardless of
background, identity, experience, or level of technical knowledge.

Participation in this project means agreeing to follow this Code of Conduct.

## Expected Behavior

- Communicate respectfully and consider how your words may affect others.
- Discuss ideas, code, and behavior without attacking people.
- Give constructive feedback and accept reasonable corrections.
- Welcome questions and help contributors learn without ridicule.
- Respect different experiences, viewpoints, and technical approaches.
- Protect privacy. Do not publish personal data, credentials, tokens, private
  messages, or production logs.
- Follow the project's contribution and security-reporting procedures.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, or deliberate exclusion.
- Insults, personal attacks, threats, or sustained disruptive behavior.
- Sexualized language, imagery, or unwanted attention.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Deliberately sharing credentials, personal data, or confidential security
  information.
- Trolling, spam, or repeatedly derailing project discussions.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to issues, pull requests, code reviews, discussions,
support channels, and other spaces managed by this project. It also applies when
someone represents the project in public, including on WordPress.org.

## Reporting a Concern

Report conduct concerns privately by emailing
[cms@support.scouting.nl](mailto:cms@support.scouting.nl?subject=Code%20of%20Conduct%20report%3A%20Scouting%20OpenID%20Connect).

Include relevant links, dates, screenshots, and a description of what happened
when possible. Do not include unrelated personal data or secrets. Reports will be
handled as confidentially as reasonably possible, but information may be shared
with the people needed to investigate and resolve the concern.

Suspected security vulnerabilities must instead be reported according to
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for interpreting and enforcing this Code of
Conduct. They may remove content, request corrective action, issue a warning,
temporarily restrict participation, or permanently exclude someone from project
spaces. The response will depend on the seriousness, impact, and recurrence of
the behavior.

Maintainers who have a conflict of interest in a report should not take part in
its investigation or resolution. Good-faith reports will not result in
retaliation. Knowingly false or malicious reports may themselves be treated as a
violation of this Code of Conduct.
